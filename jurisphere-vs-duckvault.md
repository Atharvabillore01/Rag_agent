# Jurisphere vs. DuckVault — The Definitive Competitive Comparison

Last updated: 2026-07-15

*Synthesized from four source analyses: (1) Jurisphere product/feature inventory (demo + XML twin + pricing deck), (2) Jurisphere security/infra/compliance inventory, (3) DuckVault as-built capability inventory (full codebase read), and (4) DuckVault security audit (file:line). This document is the DuckVault team's internal reference for parity, gaps, and roadmap.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

DuckVault has, as raw engineering, already built the **hard, defensible core** that Jurisphere sells — a multi-tenant, provider-agnostic, citation-grounded agentic RAG platform with genuinely strong application-layer security (per-tenant Qdrant isolation, live-DB RBAC, injection-aware grounded retrieval, a fail-fast secret validator) — plus three of Jurisphere's headline feature engines (**Parallel Review**, **Redline**, **Translation**) shipping as audited backends. Where Jurisphere is ahead is **not architecture but product surface and market assets**: a ~15M+ document real-time Indian legal corpus, fine-tuned models + a legal knowledge graph, a polished citation *scroll-and-highlight* verification UX, a List-of-Dates (chronology) builder, a live drafting assistant, Word/Office add-ins, voice input, OCR-grade Indian-language translation, and enterprise trust artifacts (SOC2/ISO27001 posture, on-prem, 8,500+ users). Critically, several of Jurisphere's most-hyped differentiators are **thinner than they look** (SOC2/ISO27001 are attributed to Azure/GCP, not to Jurisphere itself; encryption is a single unqualified sentence; no stated RBAC/SSO/MFA/audit/DPA/SLA), while several of DuckVault's *gaps* are actually **90%-plumbed seams** (multi-model task routing, Gemini/IndicTrans2 translation, fine-tuned-model hookup) that need only a key or content, not new architecture.

**Headline stat line:** DuckVault has **3 of ~9** Jurisphere feature engines fully built and audited (Parallel Review 19/19, Redline 15/15, citation-grounded Research), **2 more built-pending-key** (Translation, multi-model routing), **~6 product gaps** (LoD Builder, drafting, vision-OCR, scroll+highlight, Word add-in, voice), and **0 external-facing trust artifacts** vs. Jurisphere's ₹60k–₹1.6L/user/yr pricing, ~15M-doc corpus, 8,500+ users, and SOC2/ISO27001 (cloud-vendor) posture — but DuckVault's application-security core (tenant isolation, RBAC, injection resistance) is arguably *stronger and more verifiable* than anything Jurisphere has publicly documented.

## 4. Pricing Benchmark

Jurisphere's exact tiers (billed as **"Annual Investment per user," INR/user/year**, higher seat counts get lower per-user rates):

| Number of Users | Annual Investment / User (INR) |
|---|---|
| **Up to 10** | **₹1,60,000** |
| **11 – 25** | **₹1,20,000** |
| **26 – 50** | **₹72,000** |
| **51 – 100+** | **₹60,000** |

**Conditions / footnotes (verbatim):** "Unlimited Usage" · *"Exclusive of taxes & on-premise deployment costs"* · *"On-Premise Deployment available only above 50 logins"* · *"Exclusive of MCA Downloads which will be charged INR 200 per company and limited doctable cells"* (i.e., "unlimited" is subject to a per-company MCA charge and a doc-table-cell cap). **Additional terms:** annual payment cycle, dedicated support team, 24/7 support.

**Implications for DuckVault pricing:**
- **Anchor exists at ₹60k–₹1.6L/user/yr.** DuckVault can undercut at the SMB/solo end (Jurisphere is expensive under 10 seats at ₹1.6L) and/or match at the 50+ enterprise band with a *better* on-prem story.
- **"Unlimited usage" is asterisked** — MCA metering + doc-table-cell caps. DuckVault's genuinely usage-unbounded local-first model (subject to infra) is a cleaner "truly unlimited on your hardware" pitch.
- **On-prem gated at >50 logins** is a soft spot. DuckVault should offer **on-prem/self-host at any seat count** — the single clearest pricing wedge for data-sensitive buyers (which legal buyers are).
- **Per-seat annual** is the market-accepted model; DuckVault should mirror it for buyer-familiarity, layering usage-tier caps (which are already modeled in `RateLimitConfig`/`DemoQuota` — currently unenforced, see G8) once enforced.
- Consider a **metered add-on** analogous to MCA (e.g., premium vision-OCR/translation, or corpus access) to monetize the key-gated features once enabled.

## 6. What We Can Implement From Them — Prioritized

Each item: **value · rough effort · verifiable-now (Groq-only) vs. blocked-on-key/content.** Ordered by (value ÷ effort) with verifiability as tie-breaker.

1. **Citation scroll + highlight UX** (open doc → scroll to page → highlight exact paragraph)
   - *Value:* Very high — the single most visible parity gap and Jurisphere's flagship trust motif.
   - *Effort:* Low–medium (frontend). Backend data already persisted: `page_number`, `section_heading`, snippet per `Citation`; presigned `view-url` exists; chips already jump to `#page=N`.
   - *Verifiable now:* **Yes, Groq-only.** No key/content needed. **Do this first.**

2. **Download-to-Word (.docx) export** (research notes, review tables, redline tables, chronologies)
   - *Value:* High — table-stakes for legal deliverables; every Jurisphere output has it.
   - *Effort:* Low. python-docx already in the tree (used for parsing); add markdown/table→docx serializer + endpoints.
   - *Verifiable now:* **Yes, Groq-only.**

3. **LoD Builder / List-of-Dates chronology** (extract dated events + per-event summary + source doc + page + inline citation; Word export; firm-format)
   - *Value:* Very high — a whole module DuckVault lacks; flagged as the top parity gap; high litigation demand.
   - *Effort:* Medium. New engine (date extraction + chronology assembly) reusing the loader + citation infra; new router + page.
   - *Verifiable now:* **Yes, Groq-only** (date extraction + citation over existing chunk/page metadata). Word export from item #2.

4. **Agentic multi-sub-agent research** (planner → parallel web/statute/case sub-agents → merged note; live agent trace; follow-up questions; opinion-style formatting)
   - *Value:* High — matches their headline research UX; deepens our existing loop.
   - *Effort:* Medium (fan-out on existing planner→retrieve→verify→generate; add sub-agent sources + trace UX + follow-up generation).
   - *Verifiable now:* **Partly.** Multi-agent orchestration + follow-ups + trace = Groq-only. A **web-search sub-agent is blocked on a search API/key**; statute/case sub-agents are **blocked on corpus/content**. Ship the orchestration + tenant-doc sub-agents now; add web/corpus sources as they land.

5. **Drafting assistant** (voice/typed request → full formal legal document with parts, chronology table, quantified damages, annexure of authorities, note-to-counsel)
   - *Value:* High — Jurisphere's most impressive live demo; strong wow-factor.
   - *Effort:* Medium–high. `LLMTask.DRAFT` routing already exists (built-plumbing, unused); needs drafting engine + prompts + editable output + citations.
   - *Verifiable now:* **Yes, Groq-only** for text drafting (quality improves with a stronger DRAFT-task model via the routing seam). Voice input is a separate item.

6. **Indic translation via Gemini / IndicTrans2** (high-quality 22-Indian-language → English + OCR of scans/handwriting; side-by-side + Ask-Questions UX)
   - *Value:* High for the India legal market — direct parity with a heavily-used Jurisphere module (170+ jobs observed).
   - *Effort:* Low-code, key-gated. Engine + router + UI already **built**; `LLMTask.TRANSLATE` swaps provider via env with **no code change**; `openai_compatible` seam serves IndicTrans2.
   - *Verifiable now:* **Blocked on a key.** Text translation works on Groq now (audit pending); **quality Indian-lang + scan-OCR blocked on a Gemini/vision key or IndicTrans2 endpoint.** Also unlocks vision-OCR ingestion (fixes G17 silent-empty-image docs).

7. **Knowledge-graph grounding** (entity/citation/statute-case relationship graph; "Indian Legal Ontology")
   - *Value:* Medium–high long-term (better cross-doc reasoning + citation linking); differentiator once mature.
   - *Effort:* High. Build entity/citation extraction → graph store → retrieval integration; maintain as domain-pack content.
   - *Verifiable now:* **Partly Groq-only** (entity/citation extraction) but **the valuable statute/case graph is blocked on legal content**. Lowest priority of the seven; start with a lightweight entity-link layer.

## 7. Security Hardening Roadmap for DuckVault (Ordered)

Ordered to unblock an external pilot first (Critical infra/provisioning), then close enterprise/legal-grade requirements. IDs map to the security audit.

**Phase 0 — Block an external pilot until these are done (Critical):**
1. **G1 — Kill default infra credentials.** Force non-default MinIO / Postgres / Redis / Qdrant creds; add a startup validator (mirror the `SECRET_KEY` fail-fast pattern) that refuses to boot on `minioadmin:minioadmin`, `rag:rag`, empty Redis/Qdrant secrets.
2. **G3 — `DEBUG=false` in every non-local env.** Closes Swagger/ReDoc exposure + SQLAlchemy `echo` (PII to stdout).
3. **G2 — Secrets off-disk.** Move Groq key + `SECRET_KEY` to a managed secret store with rotation; **rotate the exposed Groq key** (it has been read).
4. **G4 — TLS everywhere between services.** HTTPS S3 endpoint, Qdrant/Redis TLS, `DATABASE_URL` with `ssl`.
5. **G7 — Fix the stream-replay IDOR.** Namespace the Redis buffer key by `tenant_id` and verify ownership in `GET /query/replay/{query_id}`.
6. **G5 — Gate registration.** Replace open self-serve `org_admin` minting with invite/allowlist + email verification; reconcile the global-email vs `(tenant_id,email)` dup-check inconsistency.

**Phase 1 — Close the "defined-but-unenforced" and auth-abuse gaps (High):**
7. **G6 — Auth throttling + lockout + failed-login audit.** Rate-limit `/auth/login` and `/auth/register`; add lockout/backoff; write failed logins to `AuditLog`.
8. **G8 — Enforce the quota machinery.** Wire `RateLimitConfig` (QPD, concurrency, ingestion/hr) + `DemoQuota` (they exist with zero callers); enforce per-day, demo, per-tenant-tier, concurrency limits.
9. **G9 — Malware/content scanning.** Activate the already-modeled `scanning`/`rejected_security` states: magic-byte sniffing + AV (e.g., ClamAV) before parse.
10. **G20 — Rate-limiter graceful degradation** when Redis is down (don't 500 `/query`).

**Phase 2 — Enterprise/legal-grade compliance (Medium):**
11. **G10 — SSO (SAML/OIDC) + MFA/2FA** (TOTP/WebAuthn). Hard requirement for legal enterprise buyers; `hashed_password` is already nullable for SSO.
12. **G11 — Encryption at rest + field-level encryption** for `Chunk.text`, review/redline/translation results, raw MinIO files; **data-retention/deletion policy** for audit PII (query text, emails).
13. **G14 — JWT hardening + revocation.** Add `iat`/`nbf`/`iss`/`aud` (and verify them); shorten lifetime; add refresh flow + denylist/revocation; consider `kid`/asymmetric for rotation.
14. **G13 — Complete audit coverage + tamper-evidence.** Write the never-written actions (`document_upload/delete`, `tenant_create`, `user_delete`, `admin_action`); capture IP + user-agent; make the audit table append-only/WORM.
15. **G15 — Password policy** (min length/complexity; guard bcrypt's >72-byte truncation; drop the `changeme123` seed default).
16. **G12 — Escape chunk *body* text** (`<`/`>`) inside the XML injection framing, not just attributes — defense-in-depth behind the prompt-level guard.

**Phase 3 — Formalize posture for certification (Medium/Low → strategic):**
17. **G21 — SOC 2 / ISO 27001 program:** data-retention/deletion policy, key-management doc, backup/DR (RTO/RPO), access-review tooling, DPA + PII inventory, non-root `Dockerfile` USER, digest-pinned images. Pursue **real org-level certification** to out-position Jurisphere's cloud-vendor-only claim.
18. **G16 — Sanitize client-facing errors** (stop streaming `str(exc)`).
19. **G17 — Surface silent image-ingestion failures** (append a warning instead of producing empty `ready` docs; fixed for free once vision-OCR lands — roadmap item #6).
20. **G18 — Replace string-based transient-retry heuristic** with typed/status-code detection.
21. **G19 / G22 — Per-user rate limiting** (not just per-tenant); consider authenticated streaming instead of bearer presigned URLs for confidential docs.
